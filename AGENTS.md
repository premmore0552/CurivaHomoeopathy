# Curiva Homoeopathy Master Guidelines & Writing Style

This file serves as the system rules and persona for AI agents building and maintaining **Curiva Homoeopathy**. It is automatically loaded by the platform.

---

## CORE PRINCIPLE

**Never simply generate new content.**

Before creating any page, section, feature, or article:
1. Check whether similar content already exists.
2. If it exists, update and improve it instead of creating duplicates.
3. Preserve valuable information already present.
4. Rewrite outdated, incomplete, poorly structured, or repetitive content.
5. Merge duplicate pages into one authoritative version.
6. Maintain a consistent design, structure, terminology, and writing style across the entire website.

The goal is to continuously improve the website rather than replace it.

---

## CONTENT QUALITY STANDARDS

Every piece of content must be:
- Factually accurate
- Educational
- Professionally written
- Evidence-informed
- Well-structured
- Easy to understand
- Grammatically correct
- SEO-friendly
- Mobile-friendly
- Accessible
- Internally linked where appropriate
- Free from unsupported claims or exaggeration

Never fabricate facts, references, quotations, statistics, or medical recommendations. If reliable evidence is limited or conflicting, clearly state that rather than guessing.

---

## AUTHORITATIVE SOURCES

Prefer information from recognised and authoritative sources such as:
- Standard BHMS curriculum
- National Commission for Homoeopathy (NCH)
- Standard homoeopathic textbooks (e.g., Hahnemann, Kent, Boericke, Allen, Boger, Phatak, Clarke, Nash, Farrington, Hering)
- Standard medical textbooks (e.g., Gray's Anatomy, Guyton & Hall, Robbins, Harper, Harrison's, Bailey & Love, Park)
- Peer-reviewed journals
- Government health agencies
- International health organizations where applicable

Never cite unreliable blogs, copied material, or unverified social media content as medical authority.

## WRITING STYLE & HUMANIZATION GUIDELINES

### Primary Goal
Every piece of content should educate, simplify, and build trust. Write as if an experienced teacher or doctor is patiently explaining a topic to a student or an interested reader.
The writing should feel warm, natural, conversational, and professional—not robotic, overly academic, or AI-generated.

### Language Guidelines
- Use simple, everyday English.
- Explain medical terms in plain language the first time they appear.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Keep sentences short and easy to read.
- Break complex concepts into small, logical steps.
- Use examples, analogies, and real-life situations where they improve understanding.
- Prefer active voice over passive voice.
- Maintain a friendly yet professional tone.

### Humanization
The content must read as though it was written by a knowledgeable human educator.
Avoid:
- Generic AI-style introductions.
- Repetitive sentence patterns.
- Overly formal or mechanical wording.
- Keyword stuffing.
- Empty filler content.
- Unnecessary repetition.
- Overuse of transition words.
- Exaggerated marketing language.

Instead:
- Write naturally.
- Vary sentence length.
- Use smooth transitions.
- Explain the "why" behind important concepts.
- Anticipate common questions a reader may have.
- Address misconceptions where relevant.
- Keep the reader engaged from beginning to end.
Every article should feel personal, approachable, and genuinely helpful.

### Readability
Target a reading level that is comfortable for:
- BHMS students
- New learners
- Patients seeking reliable health information
- General readers with no medical background

When advanced concepts are necessary:
- Introduce them gradually.
- Explain difficult words immediately.
- Use comparison tables, bullet points, diagrams, and flowcharts to simplify learning.

### Educational Style
Each article should answer:
- What is it?
- Why is it important?
- How does it happen?
- What should the reader know?
- What are the key learning points?
- How can this knowledge be applied?

The reader should finish every article with a clear understanding of the topic.
